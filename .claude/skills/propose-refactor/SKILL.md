# Propose Refactor

1. Research the codebase and identify areas that could benefit from refactoring. This involves analyzing the existing code, understanding its structure and functionality, and pinpointing areas that may be inefficient, difficult to maintain, or prone to bugs. This step is crucial for ensuring that the proposed refactor addresses the right issues and adds value to the project.
2. Propose a refactor plan for a specific part of the codebase. This includes identifying the areas that need improvement, outlining the changes that will be made, and assessing the potential impact on the overall system. 
3. Ask user to confirm the plan before proceeding with the actual refactor. This allows for feedback and adjustments to be made based on input from stakeholders and team members, ensuring that the refactor is aligned with the goals of the project and minimizes any potential risks.
4. Assessment report
- Show a score between 1 and 10 for the risk and effort of the proposed refactor, with 1 being low risk/effort and 10 being high risk/effort.
- List a file impact analysis, detailing which files will be affected by the proposed changes and how they will be impacted.
- List a UI, State, and API impact analysis, detailing how the proposed changes will affect the user interface, application state, and any APIs that are involved.

The goal is to get feedback on the proposed refactor before starting the actual implementation, allowing for adjustments based on input from stakeholders and team members.
